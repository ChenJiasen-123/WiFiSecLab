# Bellardo and Savage (2003): completed main-text reading

Source: https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~savage/papers/UsenixSec03.pdf

Read sections 1–5, the acknowledgements and reference list through the web PDF text (13 pages). This extends the previous partial reading; cited works have not thereby been read. Figure captions and textual results were read; plots were not independently digitized.

The historical paper separates identity trust failures from medium-access trust failures. Its deauthentication experiments demonstrated service disruption; its physical NAV experiments did not reproduce the predicted outcome on the tested devices, whereas simulation did. The authors discuss implementation behavior, limits of observation around hidden terminals, and tradeoffs of their proposed defenses. These are historical findings, not claims about current APs or current PMF behavior.

Platform implications: keep simulation distinct from hardware evidence; separately record constructed, submitted and independently observed frames; avoid treating silence as internal state proof. These implications motivate the new receive-driven runner's evidence fields. No DoS traffic generation or historical firmware bypass was implemented.

The main protocol, literature and platform objectives remain incomplete. This reading contributes one historical paper and does not constitute a systematic 30-year survey.
